Calibration & Tool Management System

I replaced paper grids and spreadsheet logs for equipment calibration with a mobile app and automated checks. Failed tools now lock themselves, due dates warn people ahead of time, and audit prep takes one click.

The problem

Calibration and preventive-maintenance records for the lab's instruments and test fixtures lived on paper grids and in separate spreadsheets. No one could see at a glance which tools were due, overdue, or failed. That gap is how out-of-calibration equipment ends up back on the production floor.

Audit preparation meant collecting records by hand from several places, which took hours every cycle.

What I built

1. One source of truth for every tool

A relational data model with a master inventory: each asset has an ID, a location and a lifecycle state (Active, In Calibration, Expired or Out of Service). Separate tables hold calibration measurements and routine inspection records, so the history never overlaps or gets overwritten.

2. A mobile app for the shop floor

Technicians use a phone-friendly app with four workflows:

  • External calibration certificates: log certificates from accredited labs for reference standards such as DMMs and calibrators, and reset due dates.
  • Inventory lookup: search any tool by ID, status or location.
  • Monthly PM checklist: visual and mechanical checks.
  • Internal verification: log live readings against reference values.

3. Procedures turned into enforced checks

Instead of written SOPs that rely on judgment, the forms do the checking:

  • Precision tolerances calculated in the form. Reference resistors in functional test fixtures are checked against ±0.05% windows. Low-ohm parts require 4-wire measurement.
  • Mechanical checks on bed-of-nails fixtures: pogo-pin return, board alignment and fastener condition. These catch worn hardware before it causes drift.

4. Automation that acts without being asked

  • Fail-safe lockout. An out-of-tolerance reading sets the tool to a locked status straight away and alerts the engineering lead with the details.
  • Nightly due-date scan. A Google Apps Script job checks every record at midnight. It warns 30 days ahead of external calibrations and 7 days ahead of internal PM.
  • Records open in the browser. Verification sheets link straight to Google Drive, so technicians always see the current version without downloading files.
