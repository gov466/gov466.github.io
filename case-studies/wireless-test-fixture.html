Wireless Sensor Test Fixture

A small ESP32 box that checks humidity and temperature probes from any phone in under 2 seconds. It replaced a workflow that took 2–5 minutes and needed five field loggers, a laptop and a cellular connection.

The problem

The probes are used in concrete-monitoring loggers. Verifying them meant plugging each probe into a field logger, triggering a cellular upload with a magnetic switch, waiting for the connection, then checking readings across several cloud dashboard tabs.

Each check took 2–5 minutes and needed five loggers and a laptop. Comparing readings by eye to spot a bad probe was slow and easy to get wrong.

What I built

1. A self-contained Wi-Fi test bench

An ESP32 runs as its own Wi-Fi access point, with no internet, cellular link or laptop involved. A QR code on the enclosure opens a dashboard on any phone or tablet. The dashboard is served straight from the ESP32's flash memory.

2. Several probes read at once

A TCA9548A I²C multiplexer lets one ESP32 read up to five SHT31 probes side by side. The dashboard refreshes every 2 seconds, so technicians just watch the results without tapping anything.

3. Two clear failure types

  • DISCONNECTED: no valid reading, which points to a broken wire, a detached cap or a bad connection.
  • FAIL (DRIFTED): the probe reads, but it's more than ±3.5% RH away from the batch average. That points to a contaminated sensing layer, and the probe should be scrapped.

4. A way to verify the fixture itself

The bench is checked with a saturated salt (NaCl) chamber, which holds a known 75.3% RH at room temperature. Readings must fall within 71.8–78.8% RH, which keeps the fixture's own accuracy traceable.
